# Topic 14 — Quality Strategy and Engineering Leadership

*How to set direction for quality, decide where to invest, and lead the people and culture that make it real.*

The final topic steps back from individual practices. A quality strategy is a small set of decisions: which risks matter most, what evidence shows they're under control, where effort and money go, who owns what, and how it's reviewed. Leadership is making those decisions real through people, priorities and communication.

You'll generate an evidence scorecard from everything built in Topics 1–13. It shows that the course's own risk register claimed coverage that couldn't detect a risk, and that the release gate doesn't require evidence for the riskiest feature. Then you'll write a short quality strategy for Quality Books, and a one-page update for leadership that asks for a decision.

## What you'll be able to do

- Tell a quality strategy, a test strategy, a test plan and a quality policy apart, and write a short, usable strategy.
- Ground strategy in evidence, and check that evidence actually fits each risk.
- Prioritise investments with the cost of quality and explicit trade-offs.
- Explain how the QA role is evolving, and the skills and team structures that support it.
- Build a quality culture: whole-team ownership, psychological safety, blameless learning.
- Communicate quality to leadership in business terms, ending with a decision.
