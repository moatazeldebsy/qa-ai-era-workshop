# Community

Learning alone works. Learning alongside other people works better: you see other solutions, you have to explain yours, and you get unstuck faster.

## Discussions

The course uses [GitHub Discussions](https://github.com/moatazeldebsy/qa-engineering-deep-dive/discussions):

| Category | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Topic 01** … **Topic 14** | Questions about a topic, and comparing notebooks and challenge solutions |
| **Show your work** | Link your fork, a write-up, or a talk you gave about something from the course |
| **Ideas** | Suggestions for new labs, examples or topics |
| **Study groups** | Find or start a group in your time zone or language |

When you ask a question, include the topic and step, the command you ran, its full output, and what you expected. `npm run learn:check <topic>` output is a great start.

!!! tip "Share solutions to challenges, not to labs"
    Lab reference solutions are already one `git diff` away, so posting them adds little. The wrap-up **challenges** have no reference solution on purpose, and comparing different answers to them is where discussion pays off.

## Run a study group

A format that works well:

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Before the session | Everyone reads the topic and does the lab alone |
| 0:00 | Each person shares one thing that surprised them (2 minutes each) |
| 0:15 | Walk through the quiz questions people got wrong |
| 0:30 | Two people present their challenge solutions; the group compares |
| 0:50 | Pick the next topic and a date |

One topic every one or two weeks completes the course in three to six months. Topics 1–2 are worth a session each even for experienced groups.

## Contributing

Found a mistake, a broken step or an expected output that doesn't match? Please [open an issue](https://github.com/moatazeldebsy/qa-engineering-deep-dive/issues/new/choose); the templates ask for exactly what's needed to fix it. Pull requests are welcome. See [CONTRIBUTING.md](https://github.com/moatazeldebsy/qa-engineering-deep-dive/blob/main/CONTRIBUTING.md) for how labs, checkers and expected outputs are kept honest.

Everyone taking part agrees to the [Code of Conduct](https://github.com/moatazeldebsy/qa-engineering-deep-dive/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
